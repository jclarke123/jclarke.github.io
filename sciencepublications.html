Line intensity mapping with empirical samples

Line intensity mapping (LIM) aims to determine cosmological information out to the epoch of reionisation over large sky areas. Using wide beams and frequency profiles, LIM experiments scan over the sky to measure the aggregate line emission signal from galaxies and/or the intergalactic medium. From this, one- and two-point statistics such as stacking or power spectra can be used to recover information from the underlying field, such as the galaxy luminosity function (e.g. Marcuzzo+25).

This field has been most developed in the radio regime (21cm/Hɑ), with early submm and optical experiments in various stages of development. Consequently, numerous simulation sets have been developed using hydrodyamical simulations (using EAGLE, IllustrisTNG, or similar tools) or semi-analytic treatments. However, each of these heavily rely on assumptions on what the underlying cosmology “should” look like.

My LIM simulations bypass this issue by using existing galaxy catalogues such as COSMOS2020 or Euclid as a base layer. This basis only includes value-added galaxy data detected through blind photometric and spectroscopic surveys, typically the brightest galaxies (also subject to select bias), and so is inherently incomplete due to failing to resolve dim galaxies. Consequently, these simulations are inherently tied to the existing underlying densities, and any predictions are fundamentally lower limits for detections (which are important to check when considering instrument sensitivities). From this starting point, I extrapolated to include the expected missing populations using more complete samples (e.g. COSMOS2025) as a point of comparison, included noise models, etc. By painting on line emission models to these galaxies, I made predictions that are representative of actually observed fields. These are necessary to optimally test signal cleaning techniques that will be used in observations (such as masking).

FYST/CCAT submm simulations

I primarily made these simulations for EoR-Spec Deep Spectroscopic Survey (DSS), the LIM experiment of the Fred Young Submm Telescope (FYST), part of the CCAT Observatory. EoR-Spec specifically observes the 210-420GHz range, providing information on CO line transitions at low redshift, as well as [CII] into the epoch of reionisation (3.5 < z < 8.2), both being key tracers of star formation. Building out and implementing line models for these galaxies required utilising existing samples at a given redshift, such as observations from NOEMA and the ALPINE-ALMA sample at 4.4 < z < 5.2 respectively.

To recover the target [CII], we need to identify and clean the foreground CO, as LIM cannot innately distinguish the two sources of signal. Therefore in our simulations I modelled various CO foreground removal techniques, including masking and cross-correlation. As our simulations are anchored to empirical data, any such predictions made using these techniques will be relevant for observations.

Cross-instrument analysis

The above work has been centered around FYST, however in recent months I have been expanding this to cover other submm, cm, and optical experiments: SUBLIME-TIFUUN, TIM, EXCLAIM, COMAP, and SPHEREx.

Including these other experiments is particularly relevant considering the power of cross-instrument cross-correlations at the same redshift. These cross-correlations trace the underlying matter density fields, and whilst forward modelling is required to recover the contribution of each individual line, it will be easier to get early detections from them. Such statistics have been considered in the literature before, however primarily in the context of individual transitions or experiments. Instead of looking at a handful of lines, I am currently working on a more comprehensive, holistic analysis of this across multiple instruments. By analysing the hundreds of potential cross-correlations, I will identify general trends in this regime for low- and high-redshift cross-correlations, building a case for field unification across future experiments. This work is currently in progress, so please contact me directly for more information.
